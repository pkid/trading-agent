## 10-Round Debate Script

### Round 1: Opening Statements

**Aggressive Analyst**: The portfolio is painful but not hopeless. Every holding has exposure to either AI infrastructure, semiconductors, software cash flow, or photonics optionality. If risk appetite returns, this basket can recover quickly. Sivers has explosive photonics optionality, AMD remains a core AI compute beneficiary, FORM serves the semiconductor test ecosystem, and Adobe is a high-margin software franchise trading at a depressed multiple. The portfolio is down 11.64%, but the loss is not catastrophic. I would avoid emotional selling at the lows.

**Conservative Analyst**: The problem is not the mark-to-market loss. The problem is position sizing. Sivers is 31.34% and FORM is 20.91%, yet both existing debates ended in SELL. More than half the portfolio sits in names where the risk debate says reduce. AMD is also nearly 30% and rated HOLD, not BUY. Adobe is the only clean BUY, but it is the smallest holding. That is backwards. The first move should be de-risking, not hoping.

**Portfolio Manager**: Opening score: conservative side leads. The portfolio has good themes but poor risk order. We should debate whether to preserve optionality in Sivers and FORM, but the burden of proof is on the side arguing to keep them large.

### Round 2: Concentration Risk

**Aggressive Analyst**: Concentration is not automatically bad in a small portfolio. Four positions can make sense if the investor has conviction and wants a focused book. Also, the portfolio is mostly exposed to AI and semiconductor infrastructure, one of the strongest long-term themes in the market. Diversifying away too early can dilute upside.

**Conservative Analyst**: Focus is useful when the strongest ideas are largest. Here the largest position is Sivers, a speculative name with negative earnings and financing risk, and the third-largest is FORM, which has weak technicals after a huge rerating. This is not focused quality. It is concentrated volatility. The portfolio has no stabilizer and no cash buffer.

**Portfolio Manager**: Concentration is acceptable only if sizing follows evidence. Here it does not. The book should be rebuilt around AMD and Adobe, with cash as a strategic position.

### Round 3: Sivers Semiconductors

**Aggressive Analyst**: Sivers is the wild card. It has photonics and AI-connectivity optionality, real revenue, and a stock that has captured market attention. If the story keeps compounding, selling all of it could feel terrible. Momentum names can overshoot much further than fundamentals suggest.

**Conservative Analyst**: That is exactly the danger. Sivers moved from the 3-4 SEK area to near 60 SEK intraday, while the business still has negative EBITDA, negative net income, negative free cash flow, and a very stretched valuation versus revenue. The prior debate said sell into the momentum premium and keep only a tiny speculative residual if any. At 31% portfolio weight, the position is far too large.

**Portfolio Manager**: Sivers should not be a core holding here. Decision: sell most or all. If the user emotionally wants to keep the story, cap it at 5% of the portfolio and define an exit below 44-45 SEK.

### Round 4: AMD

**Aggressive Analyst**: AMD deserves patience. The debate found strong Q1 growth, a healthy balance sheet, and real AI demand. The stock is volatile but it is a legitimate large-cap AI compute name. Selling AMD now after a pullback could mean losing the strongest institutional-quality semiconductor exposure in the portfolio.

**Conservative Analyst**: I agree AMD is not a sell, but it is also not a buy here. The single-stock debate rated it HOLD because valuation and volatility are high after a major run. At nearly 30% of the portfolio, AMD is already large enough. No new money should go there until the chart resets or breaks out cleanly.

**Portfolio Manager**: AMD is the portfolio anchor, but not an add. Hold it. If Sivers and FORM are sold, AMD weight may mechanically rise, so avoid topping it up.

### Round 5: FormFactor

**Aggressive Analyst**: FORM is a real company, not a broken concept. It has semiconductor test exposure, positive free cash flow, good liquidity, and revenue growth. Selling after a 23% portfolio loss risks locking in pain right before a recovery.

**Conservative Analyst**: The prior debate is very clear: SELL. FORM closed below important short-term and 50-day levels, showed weak momentum, and still carried demanding valuation after a massive one-year rerating. The stock can be a good company and still a bad position today. The portfolio needs to stop funding weak technicals with hope.

### Round 6: Adobe

**Aggressive Analyst**: Adobe is the best place to recycle some capital. The 10-round debate rated ADBE BUY as a disciplined starter position. It has strong margins, strong free cash flow, a reasonable multiple, and a technical repair underway. Compared with Sivers and FORM, Adobe offers a much better risk-adjusted setup.

### Round 7: Cash Versus Immediate Reinvestment

**Aggressive Analyst**: Cash can become a drag if the market rebounds. If we sell FORM and Sivers, we should redeploy quickly into Adobe or perhaps add to AMD if it stabilizes. Sitting in cash after losses may make the portfolio slower to recover.

**Conservative Analyst**: Cash is not a drag when it prevents forced mistakes. This portfolio has no defensive ballast and no dry powder. Moving from four volatile names to two stronger names plus cash is a risk upgrade. The goal is not to win back the loss tomorrow; it is to stop compounding weak decisions.

**Portfolio Manager**: Cash should be 40-50% after cleanup. That is high, but appropriate until better entries appear.

### Round 8: Loss Psychology

**Aggressive Analyst**: Selling FORM at -23% and Sivers at -10% may feel like capitulation. The investor may regret it if either stock bounces hard. A staged reduction could reduce emotional whiplash.

**Conservative Analyst**: The screenshot already shows the trap: losses are pulling attention toward recovery instead of forward risk/reward. The question is not where the positions were bought. The question is what deserves capital today. FORM and Sivers do not deserve half the portfolio today.

**Portfolio Manager**: Use staging only to improve execution, not to avoid the decision. For example: sell FORM fully now, sell at least half of Sivers now, and set a short deadline or price rule for the rest.

### Round 9: Best Concrete Trade Plan

**Aggressive Analyst**: A less drastic plan: sell FORM, keep Sivers but cut it from 31% to 10%, hold AMD, add a little Adobe, and keep the rest cash. That preserves some explosive upside while reducing the worst concentration.

**Conservative Analyst**: Better plan: sell FORM, sell Sivers to 0-5%, hold AMD, hold Adobe, and keep cash. Add Adobe only on a pullback to 247-250 or a confirmed close above 260. That plan aligns sizing with the debate ratings.

**Portfolio Manager**: The balanced version wins. Keeping a tiny Sivers stub is acceptable only if it is consciously speculative. FORM should go to zero because the debate has no near-term reason to keep it.

### Round 10: Final Vote

**Aggressive Analyst Vote**: Reduce FORM, reduce Sivers, keep AMD, add modestly to Adobe. Do not fully abandon Sivers optionality.

**Conservative Analyst Vote**: Sell FORM completely, sell Sivers almost completely, keep AMD unchanged, keep Adobe unchanged until a cleaner entry, and hold cash.

**Neutral Analyst Vote**: Rotate the portfolio away from speculative/weak technicals and toward higher-quality evidence. The optimal action is not maximum bullishness or maximum fear; it is position-size discipline.

**Portfolio Manager Final Decision**: The portfolio should be de-risked now. Sell FORM. Sell Sivers down to 0-5%. Keep AMD. Keep Adobe and add only by rule. Target 40-50% cash after the cleanup.
